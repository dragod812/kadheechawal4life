The programme

Three function days, and a ceremony that starts at about 23:00

The wedding day is built backwards from local sunset — var mala in the last of the daylight, then the dinner reception, then the ceremony straight off the end of it, from about 23:00 to 01:00, in the dark and by firelight.

The first day remains arrival-led: people can reach Puri or the Ahmedabad region through the day, and the welcome dinner has no hard start. The three function dates are 26–28 February; Monday 1 March is checkout only, after the Sunday ceremony ends.

What the space has to do

Six requirements decide whether a property can host this at all. They are the brief for anyone quoting on it — venue, planner or decorator.

01 · The ceremony ground

Open to the sky

Private, level, seating 100, and usable in full darkness. A lawn, a terrace or a courtyard — whichever the property actually has. What matters is that it is open above, that the ground is level, and that there is room for a fire at the centre of it.

02 · Sound

Quiet after midnight

Amplified sound outdoors is unlawful between 10 pm and 6 am, and the cultural-occasion permit reaches only to midnight. Starting at 23:00 puts most of the ceremony inside the part of the evening a permit can cover; what runs past midnight is the quiet end of it, and needs no PA and no microphone. The acoustic plan must work for one hundred seated guests.

03 · Fire

A controlled havan

A live sacred fire at the centre of the ceremony, approved in writing, with clearance and ventilation around it. First question to a venue, not the last.

04 · Weather cover

A real backup room

A covered space that genuinely seats 100 in the intended layout — not a floating-capacity figure. If it rains at one in the morning, there is no second plan.

05 · Rooms and roads

About 55 keys, three nights

Everyone on one property, or in an overflow within ten minutes under one hospitality desk. Plan about 165 room-nights, then replace the estimate with the assigned rooming list. Transfers must work for one hundred guests arriving through Friday.

06 · The table

Vegetarian throughout

Odia, Marathi and South Indian, across roughly nine services. No hosted bar. Proper South Indian breakfasts, and a hot service to run at 01:00.

The search

Sixty properties, ten regions

Every candidate tested against the same requirements, with its own case for and against — and every photograph opened and tagged by the part of the wedding it could actually host, because a capacity chart has never once shown us a ceremony ground.

Puri and the Ahmedabad region are now the two primary searches. Swosti Chilika leads the Puri-region list for nature and complete infrastructure, conditional on accepting the longer Ganjam transfer; Sterling and Fortune are the practical Puri-proper choices. The Chanakya BNR is the strongest Puri-proper heritage wildcard, but its 37 rooms require overflow. Madhubhan leads Ahmedabad on mature landscape and low-rise character, Taj Gandhinagar on documented capacity, and The Ummed on airport access. The comparison now uses a 55-room planning gate and 100-seat ceremony/backup test. Every other region remains visible as a research reference, not a current outreach priority.

Event language

One system, four moods

Haldi · Mehendi

Marigold shade

Ivory bases, woven rugs, floor cushions, and one dense marigold gesture. Keep proper chairs nearby for older guests.

Welcome · Day lounge

Forest floor

Use the grove for intimate conversation zones. Small accents and generous negative space keep it elegant.

Wedding · from 23:00

Protected openness

A light, engineered canopy keeps the garden feeling while solving rain and wind, and still leaves the ceremony open to the sky. Ventilation and havan clearance are non-negotiable, and at this hour every bit of light is fire, candle or lamp.

Reception

Amber reflection

Candles in glass, aged brass, and discreet tree uplighting. Lighting should feel warm and architectural, never nightclub-like.

Night accents

Burgundy, after sunset only

Keep

  • Mature trees and open sightlines
  • White vertical flowers and restrained marigold
  • Brass bells, diyas, cane, wood, stone
  • Warm amber light and candle clusters
  • Visible Odia and Marathi ritual craft
  • Fire, candle and lamp as the ceremony’s only light

Avoid

  • False palace or temple façades
  • Dense imported-flower walls
  • Cool-white or color-changing lighting
  • Mirrored sofas, acrylic furniture, chair covers
  • Large LED screens dominating the sangeet
  • Any PA or amplified audio once the ceremony passes midnight
